# Recording the synthetic gold set

Use the 30 Arabic paragraphs in [SCRIPTS.md](SCRIPTS.md). Every person and fact in them is fictional. Record consenting team members only; these scripts are not a request to collect student or parent recordings. Keep this locked gold separate from examples used for training, tuning, or prompt development.

1. Assign a speaker code such as `team-01`. Use several Egyptian Arabic speakers and share the conditions and fast/slow notes across speakers; avoid one speaker doing all noisy notes. Record consent separately in the team's approved private store, including purpose, access, withdrawal route, and the date. Put no real names or contact details in checked-in metadata.
2. Use a phone voice recorder with **mono AAC `.m4a`, ideally 16 kHz**. If the recorder cannot choose 16 kHz, keep its original recording and note the actual sample rate/channels in private metadata. Convert a private evaluation copy to mono 16 kHz in the ingestion pipeline; do not relabel the original as converted. Aim for a **20–60 second** script. Read fast notes briskly but intelligibly and slow notes deliberately, with natural Egyptian pronunciation. Target times are estimates, not measurements.
3. Make a separate short preamble recording: “تسجيل تجريبي، نص خيالي، رقم النص …، كود المتكلم …، حالة التسجيل …”. Stop, then start a fresh recording for the script alone. The evaluated file must contain only the Arabic paragraph: no preamble, roster, IDs, conditions, tags, consent statement, or commentary after it. This avoids corrupting WER/CER and leaking speaker metadata into the transcript.
4. Follow the assigned condition: `quiet` = a quiet room; `classroom_noise` = consenting team members making low background chatter without real names/details; `fan` = an ordinary fan nearby; `street` = controlled ambient street noise from a safe stationary location. Do not make incidental identifiable conversations part of the recording. Keep the phone roughly 20–30 cm from the mouth, avoid covering the microphone, and leave short natural pauses around sentences. Do not add music or deliberately distort speech.
5. Read exactly what is written. Keep the English terms; pronounce printed scores and durations naturally. Do not repair the deliberately ambiguous names, unknown names, incorrect scores, or mid-sentence self-corrections. Do not substitute real people or add personal remarks. If you miss words, make a fresh take rather than silently changing the reference.
6. Name the evaluated audio exactly `syn-001.m4a` through `syn-030.m4a`, matching `audio_file` in its JSON. Keep selected takes in a private local `evals/gold/audio/` folder, or another private directory configured for the ingestion service. All audio is ignored by git. Use a separate private metadata file for speaker code, recorder code, actual condition, delivery, take, measured duration, sample rate/channels, consent reference, and date. Replace the `to-fill` speaker/recorded_by metadata in a **private copy** of gold when evaluating recordings; retain the committed scripts as synthetic reference templates.
7. Have a second native speaker listen and check the selected take against the reference. Record any audible departure in the private human transcript. Reference spans use Unicode code points, start inclusive/end exclusive; update every affected mention offset if the transcript changes. Review facts independently of model predictions, especially shared first names, retracted statements, and scores outside 0–20. A spoken above/below-range value remains as said and blocked; no score is capped. Students not mentioned receive no inferred attendance.

No audio ships in this repository. The checked-in JSON files are text-only fictional labels and have no measured duration or real speaker count. A selftest against these references tests the evaluation harness; it is not a speech or model accuracy benchmark. Report actual consented speakers and measured durations only after recording. Native-speaker wording review and two-person annotation review remain pending in v0.
